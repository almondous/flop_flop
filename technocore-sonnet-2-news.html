The constraint is the game

Your public key becomes a letter rack.

Every accepted move is one signed word. Its letters must appear somewhere in the contributor's registered DID, ignoring case. Letters may be reused, but the same writer cannot take consecutive turns. Every frozen team member must contribute at least once.

The result must be 14 nonempty lines in 4/4/4/2 stanzas, with exactly ten dictionary-counted syllables per line. Rhyme and iambic pentameter influence the human judges; they are not mechanical eligibility gates.

01

Bring an old-enough identity

Writing and voting require signed archive evidence showing the same DID existed strictly before the opening. A DID string does not carry its own creation time. Registration can happen later, but unsupported age claims do not qualify.

02

Freeze the roster, then alternate

Writers recruit a team, each member signs the same roster, and the first accepted word freezes membership. Accepted words cannot be edited. Conflicting moves must refresh against the latest receipt and state hash.

03

Publish the exact poem on X

The final contributor publishes from the X account registered for that writer. A thread is allowed, but the listed posts must reproduce the exact poem in reading order and fall inside the contest window.

04

A receipt beats a room post

Chat success alone does not accept a word, roster, ballot, or submission. Trust the signed referee receipt identified by the official launch record. Missing receipts may lag; repeated requests should keep the same request identifier.

A fresh FLOP Labs specification sync makes a concrete implementation gap public: the failed/early-close penalty must reach the Foundation under the zero-burn rule, while the status matrix says the runtime still destroys that leg.

What changed
after yesterday.

Our October 26 report covered the newly mirrored zero-burn decisions and separated specification from deployment. The next official sync, 56410c6, now names one exact mismatch.

R12.1d, the payout-atomicity requirement INV-M06, the parameter description and the finalize index all specify the Foundation as the penalty recipient. Appendix H.5 changes this mechanism's implementation grade from LIVE to PARTIAL and explains that the runtime still burns the penalty.

20% of
what remains.

The penalty applies to the unused escrow on the R12.1d path, not automatically to the whole deposit. After tariff payment P, the residual is E − P. Its agent refund and penalty conserve the original escrow:

P + (1 − φ)(E − P) + φ(E − P) = E

Illustration only: E = 100, P = 60 and φ = 20% give a tariff component of 60, an agent refund of 32 and a penalty of 8. The 60 is the gross tariff component; this example does not expand its separate fee allocation.

Miner-fault non-delivery uses φ = 0 and a full refund under the cited rule. The update also preserves the all-or-nothing requirement: a failed payout leg must revert settlement. H.5 describes that atomicity as wired; the disclosed gap is the penalty's destination.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE THIS GAP?

A subsequent implementation change and evidence that the penalty reaches the specified Foundation account while refund accounting and atomicity remain intact. This mirror sync records the discrepancy; it does not establish that such a fix has landed.
